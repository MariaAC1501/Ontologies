\begin{abstract}
Large language models can extract scientific evidence against a fixed domain ontology or induce an ontology from example documents before extraction. A fixed ontology provides stable semantics and cross-document comparability, while an evolved ontology may capture concepts that the fixed representation omits. This study compares these strategies for full-text predictive-maintenance case extraction. It defines an evidence-grounded challenge set with a development partition and a held-out partition, then evaluates a schema-only baseline, extraction with the fixed Ontology for Predictive Maintenance Architecture and Design (\opmad), and extraction with an ontology evolved by \ontocast from development articles. A complementary human-in-the-loop experiment compares manual annotation with correction of fixed- and evolved-ontology prefills. Evaluation covers linked case accuracy, evidence support, ontology coverage and stability, annotation time, and correction effort. The study evaluates extraction and curation strategies on a deliberately heterogeneous challenge set. It does not estimate the prevalence of models or practices in predictive-maintenance literature. \operational{Results are pending corpus selection, annotation, ontology evolution, and held-out evaluation.}
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

Predictive-maintenance articles describe linked combinations of maintainable items, diagnostic or prognostic functions, condition data, and models. Recovering these combinations requires more than finding isolated entity names. An extraction workflow must identify case boundaries, link each model to the correct asset, function, and inputs, and retain evidence that supports every assertion.

A domain ontology can supply stable concepts and relation constraints for this task. \opmad represents predictive-maintenance functions, maintainable items, condition data, models, configurations, synchronization, performance information, and documentary sources~\cite{MonteroJimenez2021OPMADCBR}. Its stable vocabulary supports comparison and case-based retrieval across articles. A fixed ontology can also omit distinctions found in newer literature or impose a granularity that differs from the source text.

Ontology evolution offers a contrasting strategy. \ontocast can induce and refine an ontology from source documents while extracting facts~\cite{Belikov2025OntoCast}. An evolved ontology may improve local coverage, but its concepts can be redundant, unstable across runs, or difficult to align across documents. Comparing raw triple or class counts cannot establish which strategy produces more useful evidence.

The practical objective is also human. Scientific extraction remains useful only if a reviewer can verify and correct the result more efficiently than creating the same record manually. An ontology that improves automated metrics but increases mapping or correction effort may provide little benefit to evidence curation.

This paper therefore studies fixed and evolved ontology support at two levels. The first is a controlled full-text extraction benchmark. The second is a human-in-the-loop annotation study. Its contributions are:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item an evidence-grounded benchmark of linked predictive-maintenance cases from complete articles;
  \item a controlled comparison of schema-only extraction, fixed-\opmad extraction, and extraction using an ontology evolved from development articles;
  \item an evaluation of the evolved ontology's coverage, consistency, stability, and reuse across articles; and
  \item a comparison of manual annotation with correction of fixed- and evolved-ontology prefills.
\end{enumerate}

The study is a challenge-set evaluation rather than a systematic mapping study. It supports comparative claims about the evaluated workflows, not field-wide claims about predictive-maintenance models.

\section{Background}
\label{sec:background}

\subsection{Fixed OPMAD Representation}

\opmad was developed to support predictive-maintenance architecture design and ontology-enabled case-based reasoning~\cite{MonteroJimenez2021OPMADCBR}. The present study uses an authoritative, checksummed \opmad artifact. Specific assets and models are represented as labelled individuals. The fixed condition populates the ontology without changing its classes, properties, or axioms.

Related work constrains LLM relation extraction to ontology terms and relations to reduce unsupported output~\cite{Vukovic2024DialogueOntologyRE}, and uses dictionaries and ontologies to extract semantics from noisy maintenance records~\cite{Dixit2021MaintenanceSemantics}. Recent maintenance-report extraction also combines an ontology schema, constrained inference, and evidence localization~\cite{Zhou2026GCIEOM}. These approaches do not compare a fixed domain ontology with a development-evolved ontology on linked cases from complete scientific articles.

The benchmark focuses on the core information needed to distinguish a predictive-maintenance case: documentary source, maintainable item, maintenance function, condition data, model or coordinated model set, relations among those elements, and supporting evidence. This narrower target avoids turning fields such as heterogeneous performance values into a common effectiveness scale.

\subsection{Ontology Evolution with OntoCast}

\ontocast supports both extraction against a supplied ontology and ontology-and-fact generation from documents~\cite{Belikov2025OntoCast}. In the evolved condition, the system receives development articles without the \opmad domain seed. It induces and consolidates an ontology from those examples. The resulting ontology is frozen before held-out extraction.

Recent LLM ontology-learning work evaluates term typing, taxonomy discovery, and non-taxonomic relation extraction~\cite{Giglou2023LLMs4OL}; end-to-end work also evaluates semantic and structural graph quality~\cite{Lo2024EndToEndOL}. The present study instead tests how the ontology-construction decision changes downstream case extraction and curation.

This design tests whether an induced vocabulary transfers to unseen articles. It also separates ontology construction cost from held-out extraction cost. An ontology evolved independently for every held-out paper may improve local fit while preventing stable cross-paper comparison, so per-paper adaptive evolution is reserved for an exploratory analysis.

\subsection{Human-in-the-Loop Curation}

The assisted conditions present extracted cases and evidence in the same review interface used for manual annotation. Reviewers can add, delete, relink, or normalize values and can replace unsupported evidence. The primary human outcome is time to a record that agrees with the adjudicated reference. First-pass quality and the types of required corrections are also retained.

Ontology-guided scientific-literature extraction with human validation has been demonstrated across tasks~\cite{Chhetri2025StructSense}, while systematic-review experiments show that LLM-extracted data still need explicit human validation~\cite{Schroeder2025AIAssistedDataExtraction}. The study therefore measures correction effort and unsupported evidence, not annotation time alone.

\section{Study Design}
\label{sec:study-design}

The study contains three linked evaluations. First, all extraction conditions are evaluated against the same held-out gold records. Second, the fixed and evolved ontologies are evaluated as representations independently of fact-extraction scores. Third, a human study measures the effect of ontology-backed prefills on curation effort.

The development partition supports codebook refinement, prompt development, ontology evolution, output alignment, and annotator training. The held-out partition is sealed before the extraction configurations and evolved ontology are frozen. No held-out text or annotation can influence ontology evolution, prompt selection, normalization rules, or the crosswalk used for primary evaluation.

\subsection{Challenge-Set Corpus}
\label{sec:corpus}

The planned corpus contains approximately 32 recent open-access predictive-maintenance articles: eight development articles and 24 held-out articles. Candidate articles come from a documented broad literature retrieval, but only selected challenge-set articles undergo final eligibility verification. The challenge set is purposive and is not intended to represent publication prevalence.

An article is eligible when it reports an implemented and evaluated diagnostic or prognostic model for an engineered maintainable item and provides a usable matching full text. Reviews, conceptual architectures without an implemented model, and studies of non-engineered targets are excluded.

Maximum-variation selection covers:
\begin{itemize}
  \item diagnostic and prognostic functions;
  \item different maintainable-item types;
  \item single-model and coordinated multi-model solutions;
  \item single-case and multi-case articles; and
  \item evidence reported in prose or tables.
\end{itemize}
Selection does not use outputs from any evaluated extraction condition. PDF quality is not a selection or stratification variable. Document-processing failures are recorded as execution failures. Articles with licences that permit release of derived annotations and short evidence excerpts are preferred.

Detailed performance values and synchronization status are outside the required core benchmark. They may be retained as optional annotations when they are explicit, but they do not determine primary extraction scores.

The codebook defines how to split articles containing multiple assets, tasks, experiments, model stages, or evaluation variants. It also distinguishes an absent fact from an unclear statement, a non-applicable field, and an extraction failure. Raw source wording is retained beside every normalized value.

\subsection{Development and Gold Annotation}
\label{sec:gold}

The eight development articles are annotated and discussed during codebook refinement. The guide is frozen before held-out work begins. Held-out articles receive two independent blank-form annotations by trained reviewers who are blinded to all LLM outputs. Agreement is measured before adjudication. Disagreements are resolved against the complete article, and the resulting evidence-linked record is frozen before automated evaluation.

The unit used for uncertainty estimation is the article. All cases and assertions from an article remain together during resampling and data partitioning.

\subsection{Controlled Configuration}

All conditions use the same LLM provider and model, complete article text, document parser, sectioning policy, decoding settings, retry policy, and evidence requirements. Held-out extraction receives comparable request and token budgets. Prompt content differs only where the ontology condition requires it. The study records actual models, requests, tokens, latency, retries, failures, and software revisions.

Source bibliographic identity comes from the verified corpus manifest rather than from LLM extraction. Each article remains isolated until its cases have been constructed. The final workflow preserves page, section, and text-span provenance through extraction and normalization.

\subsection{Ontology Evolution Protocol}
\label{sec:evolution}

The primary evolved ontology is produced from the complete development texts under a prespecified article order and configuration. Ontology critique and evolution are enabled during development. Corpus-level consolidation resolves repeated or competing terms. The final graph is checked for parseability and logical or structural violations, then frozen by version and checksum.

A development-only selection rule determines the primary evolved ontology. Repeated evolution runs and alternative development-article orderings measure stability. A secondary learning-curve analysis evolves ontologies from nested subsets of two, four, and eight development articles. These analyses do not select a new ontology after held-out results are known.

A run that reaches only salvage output without satisfying the prespecified convergence and integrity criteria is a failed primary run. Salvaged graphs may be analyzed separately as implementation failures.

\subsection{Common Projection and Native Evaluation}
\label{sec:projection}

Automated extraction metrics require a common target. A crosswalk from the evolved ontology to the representation-neutral benchmark fields is developed and frozen using development data only. Terms first observed in held-out output are not silently forced into an existing field. They are flagged as unmapped and evaluated in the native-ontology analysis.

The evolved ontology is also evaluated in its own vocabulary. This prevents a fixed-ontology projection from hiding useful new distinctions. Conversely, native class and triple counts are treated as descriptive quantities rather than evidence of ontology quality.

\section{Human-in-the-Loop Experiment}
\label{sec:human-study}

The manual timings collected during held-out gold annotation provide the unassisted baseline. A stratified subset of approximately 12 held-out articles then receives two additional review sessions:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item correction of a fixed-\opmad prefill; and
  \item correction of an evolved-ontology prefill projected into the common review form.
\end{enumerate}

Reviewers assigned to an assisted session have not previously annotated that article. Roles rotate across articles so that reviewer identity is not tied to one condition. Article assignment is balanced for function, asset type, case count, and model-set complexity. Each reviewer receives the same instructions and interface, and the order of assisted conditions is counterbalanced.

The interface records elapsed time and atomic edits. Edit categories include additions, deletions, relinking, normalization changes, case splits or merges, and evidence replacement. A submitted record is compared with the frozen gold standard. Adjudicators remain blinded to the source condition when resolving any evaluation ambiguity.

The human study compares time at a stated level of quality. A faster annotation with more unsupported accepted suggestions is not treated as an improvement. A schema-only prefill may be added as a secondary human condition if the available annotation budget supports it.

\section{Evaluation and Analysis}
\label{sec:evaluation}

\subsection{Extraction Quality}

Primary extraction measures are:
\begin{itemize}
  \item entity precision, recall, and F1 for items, functions, condition data, and models;
  \item relation-level precision, recall, and F1;
  \item case-boundary and complete-case exact match;
  \item evidence-span support and entailment rates; and
  \item separate counts of hallucinated, omitted, conflated, and mislinked facts.
\end{itemize}

Matching rules for raw labels, normalized labels, coordinated model sets, and partially overlapping evidence spans are fixed on development data. Confidence intervals resample articles rather than individual assertions. Pairwise system comparisons use the same held-out articles.

\subsection{Ontology Quality}

The fixed and evolved ontologies are compared on:
\begin{itemize}
  \item coverage of the benchmark information needs and relevant competency questions;
  \item supported novel classes and relations;
  \item missing, redundant, near-duplicate, and inconsistently scoped terms;
  \item logical and structural integrity;
  \item reuse of concepts across development and held-out articles;
  \item stability across repeated runs and article orderings; and
  \item effort required to align native output with the common case representation.
\end{itemize}

Two reviewers validate claims that an evolved term is useful, redundant, unsupported, or outside \opmad. Disagreements are adjudicated using the term definition, ontology context, and source evidence.

\subsection{Human Effort and Quality}

Human outcomes include:
\begin{itemize}
  \item time to submitted annotation;
  \item time to an adjudicated correct case set;
  \item additions, deletions, relinking, normalization, and evidence corrections;
  \item unsupported suggestions accepted by the reviewer;
  \item facts omitted despite being present in the prefill or article; and
  \item field-, relation-, and complete-case quality before adjudication.
\end{itemize}

Primary comparisons are paired by article where the design permits. Analysis reports effect estimates and uncertainty intervals rather than relying only on null-hypothesis significance. Reviewer and article effects are retained in the analysis model.

\subsection{Limitations}

The challenge set is small and deliberately heterogeneous, so it cannot support estimates of field prevalence. Results depend on one domain ontology, one ontology-evolution implementation, the selected development examples, and the evaluated LLM configurations. Mapping an evolved vocabulary into a common target introduces alignment decisions. Repeated evolution runs can characterize, but cannot eliminate, stochastic and order-dependent behavior. Human-effort estimates also depend on annotator expertise, training, and interface design.
