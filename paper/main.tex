\begin{abstract}
Predictive-maintenance research reports a large and heterogeneous set of diagnostic and prognostic models, assets, and condition-data configurations. This systematic mapping study characterizes model-selection patterns in 2025--2026 English-language, open-access journal and conference articles indexed in Scopus. It uses a large language model to instantiate the existing Ontology for Predictive Maintenance Architecture and Design (\opmad) from complete article texts, then validates the workflow against independent expert annotation. The study evaluates workflow reliability; it does not claim that ontology guidance improves extraction relative to a non-ontological baseline. \operational{Final results are pending corpus construction, annotation, and validation.}
\end{abstract}

\begin{IEEEkeywords}
predictive maintenance, systematic mapping study, ontology-guided extraction, large language models, \opmad, PRISMA-S
\end{IEEEkeywords}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

Predictive-maintenance studies describe heterogeneous combinations of diagnostic and prognostic functions, maintainable systems, condition data, and models. This heterogeneity makes it difficult to reuse published evidence when selecting models for a new maintenance problem. Rather than rank models across incomparable datasets, metrics, and experiments, this study maps the reported problem--solution configurations.

The scientific objective is to map reusable model-selection knowledge from recent predictive-maintenance literature. Its methodological contribution is a human-validated workflow that uses an LLM to instantiate the existing \opmad ontology from complete article texts. \opmad represents the function, maintainable item, condition data, model, configuration, synchronization, performance indicator, and documentary source associated with a predictive-maintenance case~\cite{MonteroJimenez2021OPMADCBR}. The study populates \opmad instances rather than proposing ontology classes or properties; OntoCast is the current extraction-system basis~\cite{Belikov2025OntoCast}.

The paper does not attempt to review every aspect of predictive maintenance or extend \opmad. Its scope remains within \opmad's competency questions: predictive-maintenance functions, maintainable systems, condition data, models, model types and configurations, synchronization, performance indicators, and documentary sources.

The combined contribution is:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item a systematic evidence map of model-selection patterns in open-access predictive-maintenance diagnostics and prognostics;
  \item a human-validated method for extracting \opmad cases from complete scientific articles with an LLM; and
  \item an evidence-linked, reproducible population of the existing \opmad ontology to support future retrieval and case-based reasoning.
\end{enumerate}
The ontology itself is not a new contribution. The novelty lies in the validated population workflow, the scale and traceability of the resulting case base, and findings derived only from fields shown to be reliable.

\section{Review Scope and Research Questions}
\label{sec:scope}

\subsection{Scope}

The target corpus comprises English-language, open-access full-text journal and conference articles indexed in Scopus and published in 2025--2026. Eligible studies report at least one implemented model for an \opmad-representable diagnostic or prognostic function on an engineered maintainable item. The study is a systematic mapping study, not an effectiveness review or meta-analysis: it can characterize the solution space and identify associations or evidence gaps, but it cannot determine a universally best model because \opmad does not represent enough experimental context to make performance values comparable across studies.

Claims are explicitly limited to:
\begin{itemize}
  \item Scopus-indexed literature retrieved through a documented search;
  \item the 2025--2026 publication period and the 25 August 2026 search-date cutoff;
  \item English-language, open-access journal and conference articles; and
  \item empirical diagnostic and prognostic applications expressible using unchanged \opmad terms.
\end{itemize}
Because the search cutoff precedes the end of 2026, the incomplete 2026 portion is not interpreted as an annual trend.

\subsection{Overarching Research Question}

\begin{quote}
\emph{What model-selection patterns are reported in 2025--2026 English-language, open-access predictive-maintenance journal and conference articles indexed in Scopus for different diagnostic and prognostic functions, maintainable systems, and available condition data, and how reliably can an \opmad-guided LLM workflow recover this knowledge from their full texts?}
\end{quote}

\subsection{Analytical Focus}

PM-RQ1 maps the distribution and co-occurrence of \opmad predictive-maintenance functions, maintainable items and item types, publication years and sources, and the number of distinct cases represented by each article. PM-RQ2 examines relationships among function, maintainable item and item type, input record or data form, data variables, specific models, and the existing \opmad model-type categories: data-driven, physics-based, knowledge-based, and their combinations. This question directly addresses \opmad's intended use in representing previous cases that can inform model selection for new predictive-maintenance systems.

PM-RQ3 covers single- versus multi-model configurations, combinations of the existing model-type categories, online, off-line, or both synchronization modes, and reported performance indicators and values. Missing or unclear reporting is analyzed only when it can be distinguished from extraction failure. Detailed parallel, serial, or embedded model architectures are not inferred unless the fixed \opmad version represents them.

M-RQ1 evaluates \opmad coverage; entity accuracy for assets, variables, models, indicators, and documentary metadata; relation accuracy for the correct linkage of each model to its function, asset, inputs, configuration, and performance indicator; case-boundary accuracy; normalization accuracy; evidence grounding and hallucination rates; error in corpus-level prevalence estimates; and human annotation and adjudication effort relative to a fully manual review. It evaluates workflow reliability against an independent expert gold standard, not whether ontology guidance outperforms a non-ontological alternative. Flat-schema ablations and title/abstract-only comparisons are outside the study scope.

\section{Methods}
\label{sec:methods}

\subsection{Search and Study Selection}
\label{sec:search}

\subsubsection{Information source and search limits}

Scopus is the sole information source, accessed through its API using the same query syntax as the Scopus web interface. The final primary search (S1), reproduced in Appendix~\ref{app:scopus-search}, is the union of a maintenance/prognostic title-anchor query (S1a) and a diagnostic/condition title-anchor query (S1b). Both queries limit retrieval to English-language, open-access journal articles (document type \texttt{ar}) and conference papers (document type \texttt{cp}) published in 2025--2026. The search-date cutoff was 25 August 2026.

A pilot Scopus retrieval characterized residual out-of-field retrieval and the resulting screening workload. No sentinel set was used because the candidate literature is intentionally broad. The search strings are aligned with the diagnostic/prognostic scope without being limited to exact \opmad labels. The final reconciled S1a and S1b exports contained 1,219 and 1,642 records, respectively. Deduplication of their 2,861 source records by Scopus Paper ID and DOI produced 2,770 pre-screening candidate records. Eligibility is determined through documented title/abstract and full-text screening, not by requiring a noise-free query.

\subsubsection{Eligibility criteria}

The protocol specifies that a record is included when it:
\begin{itemize}
  \item is an English-language Scopus journal article or conference paper published in 2025--2026 and has an open-access full text that matches the bibliographic record;
  \item reports an implemented and evaluated predictive-maintenance model or coordinated model set, using data, simulation, laboratory, or operational evidence;
  \item concerns an engineered physical maintainable item or component; and
  \item addresses at least one existing \opmad diagnostic or prognostic function: fault detection, fault feature extraction, fault identification, health assessment, health modelling, one- or multiple-step future-state forecasting, or remaining useful life estimation.
\end{itemize}

A record is excluded when it is a review, editorial, book chapter, duplicate, or lacks a usable matching open-access full text. It is also excluded when its primary target is a human patient or clinical outcome, biological organism or process, software or IT service, business or social outcome, natural system, or civil/geotechnical infrastructure rather than an industrial or electromechanical asset. Finally, records describing only generic AI, control, optimization, maintenance scheduling, inventory, or conceptual architectures without an implemented diagnostic or prognostic model are excluded.

Search reporting follows PRISMA-S~\cite{Rethlefsen2021PRISMAS}; study selection will be shown in a PRISMA 2020 flow diagram~\cite{Page2021PRISMA}. The metadata-only OA-restriction sensitivity analysis in Appendix~\ref{app:oa-sensitivity} does not determine final study inclusion.

\operational{Before final screening, timestamp the protocol; define title/abstract and full-text procedures, conflict resolution, and exclusion-reason rules; retrieve and audit every included PDF for title/DOI matching and text/OCR quality; then complete the PRISMA-S report and PRISMA 2020 flow diagram.}

Specific model and maintainable-item names are represented as labelled instances rather than newly invented ontology classes. No new predictive-maintenance classes, properties, or formal taxonomy are introduced. Supporting quotations, page/section locations, extraction status, confidence, and human-adjudication status are retained in a sidecar annotation dataset or separate provenance layer. Missingness and review-workflow metadata are not encoded as new \opmad concepts.

The unit of analysis is an \opmad predictive-maintenance case, not necessarily an article. Each case binds at least one documentary source, one maintainable item, one predictive-maintenance function, the model or coordinated model set fulfilling that function, and the corresponding condition data. Model configuration, synchronization, model type, performance indicator, and performance value are associated attributes when reported. An article can therefore yield zero, one, or several cases.

\subsection{LLM-Assisted Extraction and Provenance}
\label{sec:extraction}

The final workflow processes complete article texts and instantiates \opmad cases under a frozen configuration. That configuration covers the \opmad version and extraction profile, LLM provider and exact model, prompts and decoding parameters, document parser and OCR path, sectioning and chunking policy, retry and failure policy, normalization rules, and software revisions. Each analytical assertion retains a link to its supporting text span. Outputs remain isolated by article until corpus-level cases are constructed.

\subsection{Human Gold Standard and Validation}
\label{sec:validation}

A development sample establishes the annotation guide, prompts, and pipeline. A separate held-out sample is stratified by function, asset type, model type/configuration, article complexity, publication year, and PDF/OCR quality. Annotators record complete cases and supporting evidence rather than isolated labels. Independent double annotation and expert adjudication are used where feasible, and human agreement is measured before evaluating the LLM workflow.

Evaluation reports field- and case-level performance with uncertainty intervals. Metrics include precision, recall, F1, multi-label agreement, exact-match accuracy for controlled values, relation-level F1, evidence-entailment rate, complete-case accuracy, and absolute error in aggregate corpus estimates. It also distinguishes hallucinated, conflated, mislinked, and omitted facts, and measures the human annotation and adjudication effort relative to a fully manual review.

Acceptance criteria are prespecified before held-out evaluation. A domain result is reported from automatic extraction only when its required fields meet those criteria; otherwise, affected fields are manually completed, clearly qualified, or excluded from the corresponding predictive-maintenance question. The methodological objective is to establish whether the workflow is reliable enough for assisted evidence synthesis, not to conduct an ontology-benefit ablation or a title/abstract-only comparison.

\operational{Before held-out evaluation, set the sample size, field-specific acceptance criteria, confidence-interval/resampling procedure, and detailed adjudication process.}

\subsection{Predictive-Maintenance Synthesis}
\label{sec:synthesis}

The synthesis reports study and case counts separately. It summarizes systems, functions, condition data, models, and model types; analyzes cross-tabulations and association patterns among problem and solution attributes; compares single- and multi-model use across functions and assets; and summarizes synchronization and performance-indicator reporting. Sparse combinations are interpreted only after checking extraction recall and corpus coverage. Sensitivity analyses address extraction error and the open-access corpus boundary.

The synthesis may identify approaches that are common or uncommon in particular contexts, but it does not rank models by effectiveness across incomparable datasets, metrics, and experiments.

\section{Open-Access Restriction Sensitivity Analysis}
\label{app:oa-sensitivity}

This appendix reports the final metadata-only OA-restriction sensitivity analysis, not an included-study analysis. S2a was formed by deleting only the leading \texttt{OPENACCESS(1) AND} predicate from final S1a. S2b was formed the same way from final S1b; it was retrieved as disjoint 2025 and 2026 partitions after the unpartitioned API request reached its 200-page rate ceiling. The partition totals summed exactly to the unpartitioned S2b source total.

Every record had a Scopus Paper ID, DOI, title, year, and abstract. Repeated API passes were reconciled by Paper ID only when their unique-ID union equalled the stable source total: three passes for S1a, four for S2a, five for the 2025 S2b partition, and three for the 2026 S2b partition. Thus, recovery passes did not increase the reported source counts. ``Non-OA'' below denotes the complement of the matched OA set in the all-access S2 universe, not a separately verified licence classification.

\subsection{Textual similarity and topic markers}

No author or indexed keywords were available in the exports, so this analysis used title-plus-abstract text. After removal of generic scientific and query wording, unigram/bigram document-frequency profiles had high cosine similarity (0.988; 1.0 is identical), Jensen--Shannon divergence of 0.275 (0 is identical), and overlap of 17 of the 20 and 89 of the 100 most prevalent terms. A five-fold held-out text-only OA-status classifier had AUC 0.66 and balanced accuracy 0.61 (0.50 is chance). Within the maintenance/prognostic and diagnostic/condition strata, AUC was 0.63 and 0.66, respectively. Thus, the candidate sets have broad topical overlap but are not textually interchangeable.
