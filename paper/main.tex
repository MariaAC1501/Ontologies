\begin{abstract}
Predictive-maintenance research reports a large and heterogeneous set of diagnostic and prognostic models, assets, and condition-data configurations. This systematic mapping study will characterize model-selection patterns in 2025--2026 English-language, open-access journal and conference articles indexed in Scopus. It will use a large language model to instantiate the existing Ontology for Predictive Maintenance Architecture and Design (\opmad) from complete article texts, then validate the workflow against independent expert annotation. The study evaluates workflow reliability; it does not claim that ontology guidance improves extraction relative to a non-ontological baseline. Final results are pending corpus construction, annotation, and validation.
\end{abstract}

\begin{IEEEkeywords}
predictive maintenance, systematic mapping study, ontology-guided extraction, large language models, \opmad, PRISMA-S
\end{IEEEkeywords}

\subsection{Search and Study Selection}
\label{sec:search}

\subsubsection{Information source and search limits}

Scopus is the sole information source, accessed through its API. The final primary search (S1), reproduced in Appendix~\ref{app:scopus-search}, is the union of a maintenance/prognostic title-anchor query (S1a) and a diagnostic/condition title-anchor query (S1b). Both queries limit retrieval to English-language, open-access journal articles (document type \texttt{ar}) and conference papers (document type \texttt{cp}) published in 2025--2026. The search-date cutoff was 25 August 2026; the protocol archives the exact API execution times, complete executed queries, and applied filters.

No sentinel set was used because the candidate literature is intentionally broad. The final reconciled S1a and S1b exports contained 1,219 and 1,642 records, respectively. Deduplication of their 2,861 source records by Scopus Paper ID and DOI produced 2,770 pre-screening candidate records (Appendix~\ref{app:pre-screening}). Eligibility remains determined through documented title/abstract and full-text screening, not by requiring a noise-free query.

\subsubsection{Eligibility criteria}

The following criteria will be frozen before screening. A record will be included when it:
\begin{itemize}
  \item is an English-language Scopus journal article or conference paper published in 2025--2026 and has an open-access full text that matches the bibliographic record;
  \item reports an implemented and evaluated predictive-maintenance model or coordinated model set, using data, simulation, laboratory, or operational evidence;
  \item concerns an engineered physical maintainable item or component; and
  \item addresses at least one existing \opmad diagnostic or prognostic function: fault detection, fault feature extraction, fault identification, health assessment, health modelling, one- or multiple-step future-state forecasting, or remaining useful life estimation.
\end{itemize}

A record will be excluded when it is a review, editorial, book chapter, duplicate, or lacks a usable matching open-access full text. It will also be excluded when its primary target is a human patient or clinical outcome, biological organism or process, software or IT service, business or social outcome, natural system, or civil/geotechnical infrastructure rather than an industrial or electromechanical asset. Finally, records describing only generic AI, control, optimization, maintenance scheduling, inventory, or conceptual architectures without an implemented diagnostic or prognostic model will be excluded.

Search reporting will follow PRISMA-S~\cite{Rethlefsen2021PRISMAS}; study selection will be shown in a PRISMA 2020 flow diagram~\cite{Page2021PRISMA}. A metadata-only OA-restriction pilot is reported in Appendix~\ref{app:oa-sensitivity}; it does not determine final study inclusion.

\section{Open-Access Restriction Sensitivity Pilot}
\label{app:oa-sensitivity}

This appendix reports a metadata-only pilot, not a final included-study analysis. On 25 August 2026, two OA-filtered Scopus API queries were run: a prognostic title-anchor query (S1a) and a diagnostic title-anchor query (S1b). The corresponding all-status queries (S2a and S2b) differed only by deletion of the leading \texttt{OPENACCESS(1) AND} predicate. The complete exports and their hash markers are retained in the study repository.

Every exported row had a Scopus Paper ID, title, and abstract. Paper ID was used for deduplication. The S1a and S1b results overlapped by 27 records; the S2a and S2b results overlapped by 85. S2a contained seven repeated Paper IDs and S2b contained four. One OA diagnostic record was absent from the S2b export obtained a few minutes later; it was excluded only from the matched comparison below. Thus, ``non-OA'' denotes the complement of the OA-filtered set in the matched all-status candidate universe, not a separately verified licence classification.

\subsection{Textual similarity and topic markers}

No author or indexed keywords were available in the exports, so this analysis used title-plus-abstract text. After removal of generic scientific and query wording, a unigram/bigram document-frequency comparison showed high overlap: cosine similarity was 0.977 (1.0 indicates identical profiles), Jensen--Shannon divergence was 0.022 (0 indicates identical profiles), and 18 of the 20 most prevalent terms overlapped. A five-fold held-out text-only classifier distinguished OA status only modestly (AUC 0.65; balanced accuracy 0.61, where 0.50 is chance). The corresponding AUCs within the prognostic and diagnostic search-anchor strata were 0.63 and 0.64.

Table~\ref{tab:oa-topic-markers} gives selected multi-label markers. Each marker was counted at most once per record. The term differences are descriptive of the retrieved candidate records; they are not screening decisions, OPMAD annotations, or claims about model effectiveness.

The largest difference is a roughly ten-percentage-point lower prevalence of RUL language in OA records. It persists within the prognostic-anchor-only stratum (65.9\% OA versus 74.4\% non-OA). The evidence therefore supports describing the OA set as a broadly comparable but incomplete topical subset, not as a wholesale thematic skew.

An OA-only final corpus is appropriate if the review's claims remain explicitly limited to English-language, Scopus-indexed OA studies. It cannot support field-wide prevalence claims.

This pilot also cannot freeze the final corpus: it contains residual out-of-scope noise, has not applied the implemented-model and engineered-item eligibility criteria, and does not verify usable full text or OA licences. The final protocol must repeat the sensitivity comparison after the frozen search, deduplication, screening, and full-text verification.
