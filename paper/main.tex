\begin{abstract}
Predictive-maintenance research reports a large and heterogeneous set of diagnostic and prognostic models, assets, and condition-data configurations. This systematic mapping study will characterize model-selection patterns in 2025--2026 English-language, open-access journal and conference articles indexed in Scopus. It will use a large language model to instantiate the existing Ontology for Predictive Maintenance Architecture and Design (\opmad) from complete article texts, then validate the workflow against independent expert annotation. The study evaluates workflow reliability; it does not claim that ontology guidance improves extraction relative to a non-ontological baseline. Final results are pending corpus construction, annotation, and validation.
\end{abstract}

\begin{IEEEkeywords}
predictive maintenance, systematic mapping study, ontology-guided extraction, large language models, \opmad, PRISMA-S
\end{IEEEkeywords}

\subsection{Search and Study Selection}
\label{sec:search}

\subsubsection{Information source and search limits}

Scopus is the sole information source, accessed through its API. The final primary search (S1), reproduced in Appendix~\ref{app:scopus-search}, is the union of a maintenance/prognostic title-anchor query (S1a) and a diagnostic/condition title-anchor query (S1b). Both queries limit retrieval to English-language, open-access journal articles (document type \texttt{ar}) and conference papers (document type \texttt{cp}) published in 2025--2026. The search-date cutoff was 25 August 2026; the protocol archives the exact API execution times, complete executed queries, and applied filters.

No sentinel set was used because the candidate literature is intentionally broad. The final reconciled S1a and S1b exports contained 1,219 and 1,642 records, respectively. Deduplication of their 2,861 source records by Scopus Paper ID and DOI produced 2,770 pre-screening candidate records. Eligibility remains determined through documented title/abstract and full-text screening, not by requiring a noise-free query.

\subsubsection{Eligibility criteria}

The following criteria will be frozen before screening. A record will be included when it:
\begin{itemize}
  \item is an English-language Scopus journal article or conference paper published in 2025--2026 and has an open-access full text that matches the bibliographic record;
  \item reports an implemented and evaluated predictive-maintenance model or coordinated model set, using data, simulation, laboratory, or operational evidence;
  \item concerns an engineered physical maintainable item or component; and
  \item addresses at least one existing \opmad diagnostic or prognostic function: fault detection, fault feature extraction, fault identification, health assessment, health modelling, one- or multiple-step future-state forecasting, or remaining useful life estimation.
\end{itemize}

A record will be excluded when it is a review, editorial, book chapter, duplicate, or lacks a usable matching open-access full text. It will also be excluded when its primary target is a human patient or clinical outcome, biological organism or process, software or IT service, business or social outcome, natural system, or civil/geotechnical infrastructure rather than an industrial or electromechanical asset. Finally, records describing only generic AI, control, optimization, maintenance scheduling, inventory, or conceptual architectures without an implemented diagnostic or prognostic model will be excluded.

Search reporting will follow PRISMA-S~\cite{Rethlefsen2021PRISMAS}; study selection will be shown in a PRISMA 2020 flow diagram~\cite{Page2021PRISMA}. A final metadata-only OA-restriction sensitivity analysis is reported in Appendix~\ref{app:oa-sensitivity}; it does not determine final study inclusion.

\section{Open-Access Restriction Sensitivity Analysis}
\label{app:oa-sensitivity}

This appendix reports the final metadata-only OA-restriction sensitivity analysis, not an included-study analysis. S2a was formed by deleting only the leading \texttt{OPENACCESS(1) AND} predicate from final S1a. S2b was formed the same way from final S1b; it was retrieved as disjoint 2025 and 2026 partitions after the unpartitioned API request reached its 200-page rate ceiling. The partition totals summed exactly to the unpartitioned S2b source total.

Every record had a Scopus Paper ID, DOI, title, year, and abstract. Repeated API passes were reconciled by Paper ID only when their unique-ID union equalled the stable source total: three passes for S1a, four for S2a, five for the 2025 S2b partition, and three for the 2026 S2b partition. Thus, recovery passes did not increase the reported source counts. ``Non-OA'' below denotes the complement of the matched OA set in the all-access S2 universe, not a separately verified licence classification.

\subsection{Textual similarity and topic markers}

No author or indexed keywords were available in the exports, so this analysis used title-plus-abstract text. After removal of generic scientific and query wording, unigram/bigram document-frequency profiles had high cosine similarity (0.988; 1.0 is identical), Jensen--Shannon divergence of 0.275 (0 is identical), and overlap of 17 of the 20 and 89 of the 100 most prevalent terms. A five-fold held-out text-only OA-status classifier had AUC 0.66 and balanced accuracy 0.61 (0.50 is chance). Within the maintenance/prognostic and diagnostic/condition strata, AUC was 0.63 and 0.66, respectively. Thus, the candidate sets have broad topical overlap but are not textually interchangeable.

An OA-only corpus is appropriate only if claims remain explicitly limited to English-language, Scopus-indexed OA studies; it cannot support field-wide prevalence claims.
